How the money works

Roughly 1% of every trade is routed into a community treasury, held in SOL. That treasury pays the people who do the work — replies, memes, mods, clips, code — and every payout is on-chain and auditable. It is the thing MYCEL exists to prove, and it is the reason anyone outside the community should care about a coin this size.

The important number is not the balance, it is the flow. At today's volume the treasury takes in about 0.7 SOL a day — roughly 5 SOL a week, and that grows in lockstep with volume. The ~10 SOL sitting there now is seed capital to get the crew started; after that, the crew is paid out of the flow they create. That is the whole flywheel, and it is why the scoreboard tracks SOL per day and nothing else.

Two things that are not true yet

The treasury wallet does not exist, and nothing has routed. Until both are real and public, everything above is an intention, and we should describe it that way — "here is what we are setting up" rather than "here is what happens." Creating the wallet and routing the first payment are the first two items in Track A and Track C for exactly that reason. The moment they are done, this becomes the strongest claim any coin at our size can make. Until then it is a promise, and promises are what everyone else already has.

The one thing to understand before executing any of it

Replies do not buy volume. Replies buy contributors. Contributors buy volume. Converting reach into trades at the rates a small account actually gets would need tens of thousands of profile visits a day to add a few hundred trades — it does not work. What works is that Track B finds people, Track C pays them and requires them to hold, and each of those people becomes a buy, a holder, a poster and a recurring trader. Track B is a recruiting funnel wearing a marketing costume.

Track A

Standing

Cheap, one-time, and it removes the objections that otherwise end every conversation before it starts. Most of it is publishing things that are already true.

A1 · Create the treasury wallet and publish the address

A dedicated wallet, separate from every other wallet, that receives the community share of trade fees. Publish the address on day one, before there is anything in it. A treasury anyone can watch is worth an order of magnitude more than a treasury we describe, because it turns every future payout into something the audience verifies rather than something they take our word for. It also means the balance itself becomes a live scoreboard we never have to update by hand.

A2 · Publish the fee structure once, in full

State plainly how trade fees split — what goes to the community treasury, and that a creator/platform share exists. Not amounts. Structure.

This matters more than it looks. An undisclosed creator share that someone discovers later is fatal at our size; a disclosed one is completely normal and nobody blinks. Declaring the shape now is what makes it legitimate to take a creator share openly at any point in the future — the disclosure has to come before it matters, not after someone asks.

A3 · Publish the two-year lock

10% of supply is locked for two years and almost nobody knows. "The dev will dump" is the first thing every stranger thinks about a coin this size, and we have a verifiable on-chain answer we are not using. A lock nobody can check is worth nothing — this needs a public address or transaction, and then it lives in the bio and the pinned post permanently.

Be precise about the wording. "10% of total supply" and "10% of the founder's allocation" are very different claims, and overstating it once would destroy the exact trust this whole plan runs on. Publish the smaller true version.

A4 · Lock the LP and publish that too

Locked liquidity counts in our favour in Jupiter's assessment and independently removes the second-biggest objection after the dev dump. Same treatment: address, link, pinned.

A5 · The verification stack

"Getting verified" is a stack, not a switch — and the top of it is bought by Track B, which makes this the cleanest link between the X grind and something a trader can actually see.

  1. Check ticker collisions first. Other Mycel-named projects exist, and duplicate tickers are among the most common rejection reasons. Know where we stand before applying.
  2. Confirm the authority facts. Mint and freeze revoked, metadata immutable — we already pass this. Screenshot for the diligence page.
  3. DexScreener Enhanced Token Info — from $299, processed in under 15 minutes. Puts our description, socials and links on the page every trader lands on before buying. Check whether earlier promotion spend already covered this before paying twice.
  4. File CoinGecko and CoinMarketCap. Free, slow. Worth filing now — DexScreener pulls token info from external lists including CoinGecko, so a listing propagates.
  5. Jupiter verification, last. The green check marks us as the canonical MYCEL wherever Jupiter routes. Their criteria: social support — "Smart Followers" and organic engagement on an X account dedicated to the project, not a personal or KOL account — plus ticker uniqueness, on-chain liquidity (locked liquidity counts), and revoked authorities. Social proof is the gate, and Track B is how we pass it. Do not spam re-applications; fix the gap first.

A6 · The diligence page

Buyers with real size do not get convinced, they screen. They check the same handful of things every time, and we can answer all of them with a link:

  1. Supply concentration — holder distribution, no bundled clusters
  2. Can the founder dump — the two-year lock, with its address
  3. Is liquidity locked — the LP lock, with its address
  4. Can supply be inflated or frozen — revoked mint and freeze authorities
  5. Where do fees go — the treasury wallet address and the published split
  6. Is anyone accountable — a doxxed founder who streams weekly
  7. Is there a product — Organic, the rail, and its auditable payouts

One pinned post or page, kept current. An afternoon of work. It is what we point at in every FUD reply and every conversation with someone holding real size — and then the pitch is not a pitch: "here are the seven things you were going to check, already checked." Nobody at our size writes this, which is exactly why it works.

Trending lists are for eyeballs, not for alpha

By the time a token tops DexScreener trending it has usually done 60–80% of its move, because trending ranks on 24h volume and recent price action — the signal arrives after the price does. That does not make trending useless to us. We are not there to buy; we are there because the reply section of a trending coin is the densest concentration of active Solana traders available at that moment. Engage the room, ignore the chart.

B3 · Post formats — the five that rotate

  • The Ledger (daily) — treasury balance, fees routed to date, wallets paid, tx links. Post the zeros too. A project that publishes its own zeros earns the right to be believed about its non-zeros.
  • The Receipt (weekly) — someone worked, someone got paid, here is the transaction. Screenshot in the main tweet, link in reply 1, tag the contributor.
  • The Lore drop (2–3×/week) — mushroom, network, substrate, the four chapters. Visual, short, unexplained.
  • The Take (daily) — an opinion about fee models, contributor pay or the trenches that does not mention $MYCEL. A coin account that only talks about its own coin has a follower ceiling equal to its holder count.
  • The Ask (2–3×/week) — small and answerable. Not bare "gm" — "gm. one open bounty today: ___".

Format rules

  • No external link in the main tweet — roughly 30–50% reach cut. Link in reply 1.
  • Native media only, uploaded to X, never a link preview.
  • Zero to two hashtags. Three or more trips spam filtering; X ranks semantically now.
  • Threads: hook tweet alone, no link, no image if the hook is the point.
  • Reply to our own post once at ~20 minutes with the link or an addition.
  • Post inside the strike window so the roster is awake to give first-15-minute velocity.

Reply quality — the only rule

A reply must add a fact, disagree specifically, tell a one-sentence story, or be funnier than the post. If it does none of those, don't send it. Our first 500 replies are our entire public reputation.

Coordinating five accounts without losing them

X treats coordinated activity across multiple associated accounts as platform manipulation, and the March–April 2026 enforcement wave suspended thousands of accounts including, by X's own later admission, many legitimate ones. The 50k ally is the most valuable and most exposed asset we have.

Never more than two accounts under the same post, and only with genuinely different angles. Never identical or near-identical text. Stagger timing — no two accounts replying to the same target within minutes. No scheduled reciprocal like-or-retweet rings. Keep the accounts on the separate people and devices they already belong to. And when the raid bot arrives, never pay for likes — they are the cheapest action to fake and the most obviously patterned when a group performs them on cue.

Track B · Runbook

The FUD protocol

FUD is not an attack to survive, it is the highest-intent audience we will ever get: people already thinking about the coin, in public, with an audience watching. Handled well it is our best conversion surface. Handled badly it is the end.

Classify before typing

  1. Factually wrong claim "mint isn't revoked" · "dev holds 40%" · "liquidity isn't locked" Correct once, with a link, calmly. This is what the diligence page is for — one URL answers most of them. Then stop.
  2. Opinion or vibe "looks dead" · "another mushroom coin" Either ignore it, or agree and differentiate. Arguing with a vibe makes us look small and gives it oxygen.
  3. Legitimate criticism "volume is down 99%" · "nothing has routed yet" Concede first, publicly, faster than they can press it. Then say what we are doing about it and by when — and then actually do it. This is the single most credibility-building move available to us, and it costs nothing because the criticism was true anyway.

The rules, in order of how expensive they are to break

  • Never argue twice. One answer, with evidence. If they come back, let the answer stand — the audience already saw it, and round two only makes us look rattled.
  • Never delete, never block a critic. Screenshots outlive us, a deleted reply becomes the story, and blocking is read as an admission.
  • Answer with a link, never with adjectives. "It's not a rug, we're legit" is worthless. An address is not.
  • Speed matters as much as content. Inside 15 minutes. FUD that goes unanswered for an hour becomes the accepted version.
  • The coin account stays flat; the founder account can be human. Split the emotional register — the network does not get defensive, the person can be annoyed.
  • If the FUD is true, publish it ourselves first. Being the fastest source of our own bad news is the cheapest credibility there is, and it permanently removes the weapon.

Turn the answer into content

Every good FUD answer is a post. Take the question, strip the attacker, publish the answer as a standalone Take: "Someone asked whether the founder can dump. Here's the lock, here's the address, here's when it opens." We get the same content from an attack that we would otherwise have had to invent — and the next person who wonders finds the post instead of the argument.

Track C

The rail

The actual growth engine. Everything in Track B exists to feed this.

C1 · Route the first payment — this week

Right now the story is that fees route to people who work for the coin and that nothing has routed yet. That sentence is an unfired gun. "Here is the first person ever paid by a coin for working on it — tx link" travels outside our community, gets quoted in fee-economics arguments, and recruits in the replies. It converts a claim into a receipt, and receipts are our only durable content advantage. Do it before starting the daily grind — every archetype above is stronger with one behind it.

C2 · The rewards page on Organic

The onboarding and rewards page being built inside Organic is the home for all of this: contributors sign up there, work is tracked there, and rewards are calculated from volume and fees there. That makes it more than campaign infrastructure — it is the product demo. Every contributor who gets paid through it is simultaneously a MYCEL holder and a live proof that Organic's rail works, which is exactly the case Organic needs to make to the next project that launches on it.

Ship the crude version first and in public. A build thread about paying people from trade fees will out-perform any meme we make this month, because it is a claim nobody else can make and everyone in our niche has an opinion about it.

C3 · How the budget actually works

The treasury holds ~10 SOL of seed capital. That is not the budget — the flow is. At current volume roughly 5 SOL a week arrives, and that scales directly with the ladder. So the crew starts small and grows as the volume they create grows, which is the only funding model that does not run out.

  • Most of the inflow → contributor payouts. The half that compounds.
  • A standing reserve → liquidity. Liquidity is the ceiling on trade size — at a $20.8k pool a $1,000 buy is ~5% of the pool and prices like it. Deepening it raises how much volume the pool can host before slippage scares size away. It is capital at risk in an LP position, so size it deliberately.
  • A small slice → event amplification, spent only behind a receipt: the first payout, a contributor receipt, the lock announcement.

The benchmark that settles every future spend: ~$100 per durable holder — a contributor who holds, posts and trades. Anything that cannot plausibly beat that is worse than simply routing the same SOL through the rail. Judge boosts, KOLs and giveaways against that line, not against how they feel.

What we already learned about paid promotion

We ran a paid-promotion test early on — boosts and placement. The result of that spend is the baseline at the top of this page. That is not a wasted experiment, it is the cleanest evidence we will get about what the channel buys: impressions on a surface populated largely by bots and snipers, converting into trades that stop when the budget stops.

The rule it gives us: at a 1% take, any spend has to induce a hundred times its own value in volume just to break even. Boosts stay in the plan only as amplification behind a real event — never as standalone spend. And before spending again, pull the attribution: we know the dates, and DexScreener keeps the volume history.

C4 · The token gate — hold duration, not balance

Average hold time on Solana meme trades is measured in seconds, so a gate that checks balance at the moment of joining is close to meaningless — people buy in, join, sell, and stay. Two decisions fix it: gate on minimum balance held for a minimum number of days, and re-verify continuously with removal on sell, announced in advance and applied without exception. The gate only works if losing access is real.

C5 · Raid bot — write the scoring before the money

The moment engagement pays, we have created a farm. Sybil resistance is a trade-off between openness and abuse, and the answer is several weak barriers rather than one strong one. Retrofitting these means taking money away from people, which is worse than never offering it.

  • Never pay for likes. Pay for replies that earned a reply back, and for quote-posts.
  • Weight by target size. A reply under a 100k post is worth many under a 500-follower one, or the farm optimises for dead rooms.
  • Earning requires the hold gate from C4. That makes the bot and the gate one system, and prices sybil identities above what they can earn.
  • Cap weekly payout per wallet below the cost of maintaining a credible second identity. The cap is our sybil economics.
  • Decay scores over time so early contributors cannot rent-seek forever.
  • Human review of top earners before every payout. Five minutes a week; no automated system replaces it.
  • Publish the leaderboard and the payouts. The scoreboard is the content — the Ledger post writes itself once this exists.

C6 · Pay in MYCEL, bought on the market

The treasury holds SOL. Using it to buy MYCEL and paying contributors in that makes every payout a trade — which at ~1% partially refunds the treasury, while putting the token in the hands of the people doing the work under the same hold requirement as everyone else.

Two conditions. Pair it with vesting or a minimum hold on the paid tokens, or we have built a faucet that sells into our own pool every week. And describe the mechanism plainly rather than marketing it as a price mechanism — "fees buy the token" has regulatory texture in some jurisdictions. Take advice before scaling it.

Track D

Being doxxed is a strategy, not a fact

We compete against anonymous deployers in a market where the default assumption is that the founder disappears. Every stream where a real face is attached to a commitment, and every commitment then kept on a public timestamp, compounds into the one asset none of them can copy. Use it deliberately: make dated promises small enough that we always keep them, then keep them in public. Six kept small promises beat one big announcement.

What not to run

  • Giveaway streams. Attracts exactly who we don't want.
  • Countdown "big reveal" events with nothing behind them. Countdowns train people to sell at the top of the countdown.

Appendix

Three questions with hard answers

Settled, not matters of taste. Getting any of them wrong is expensive.

"Every coin is pumping — are they all insiders?"

Partly, and less than it looks. Deployer-funded same-block sniping — where the deployer funds the wallets buying in the launch block — is a normalised playbook run thousands of times a week on Solana, and bundled launches where insiders take a majority of supply and sell six figures within hours are routinely documented.

But nobody knows the share, and the honest researchers say so. There is no credible public figure for how much launch-moment buying is insider or automated: on-chain data cannot separate a sniper from an insider from a well-configured retail bot, because wallets rotate and funding is laundered through intermediaries. Treat any quoted percentage as unfounded.

The bigger distortion is survivorship. We never see the thousands of coins that died quietly today. Scanners sort by gainers, the algorithm shows what is moving, and peers post their wins. The feed is a minute-by-minute highlight reel of the top 0.1% of outcomes — not evidence that pumping is normal. We cannot out-manufacture the manufactured coins, and the position that is available to a doxxed team with a product is being visibly not that, with the evidence trivially checkable. The traders with real size all got burned by the other kind and are actively screening for it.

"Can we market-make our own coin, since the fees come back?"

No — and the arithmetic kills it before the law does. The treasury receives ~1% of volume. To generate that volume ourselves we pay the pool's fee on every swap. At absolute best we receive back the fees we just paid, minus the LP's share, minus priority fees, minus what real traders take off us. There is no version where it profits.

Projects do it anyway for perception — liquidity looks like interest and interest attracts real flow. That is exactly the conduct the DOJ and SEC charged multiple "market making" firms with across 2024–2026, including an FBI operation that ran its own token to identify firms selling manipulation as a service; firms were fined and individuals sentenced. And on our facts it is self-defeating a third way: wash patterns are what Jupiter's review and every serious buyer's screening look for. We would be paying to acquire the signature that disqualifies us from the standing Track A is building.

"So what do market makers actually do, and what can we legitimately do?"

Legitimate market making is quoting both sides and absorbing flow: posting orders real traders fill, tightening the spread, deepening the book so size can move without wrecking price. Deepening liquidity is not manipulation in any market — the illegality lives entirely in trading with yourself to fake activity.

At our size no reputable market maker will engage; the firms that will are overwhelmingly the manipulation-as-a-service kind. The legitimate version available to us is providing liquidity in our own pool — capital in, fees from other people's real trades, and a higher ceiling on how much volume the pool can host before slippage scares size away. That is the liquidity reserve in C3, and its cost is real: impermanent loss and locked capital.
